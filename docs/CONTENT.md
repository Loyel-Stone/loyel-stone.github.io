## Further developing the SayRight case study

Prefer one specific, verifiable narrative to a long feature list. Supply only sanitised materials explicitly approved for publication. No source repository access is needed to begin: the owner can provide approved excerpts, screenshots and summaries.

1. **Problem:** a first-hand situation; intended learner; existing limitation. Mark hypotheses as hypotheses. Do not generalise a personal experience into user research.
2. **Product Vision:** intended value, reason for AI, product boundary and a future success criterion. Distinguish ambition from proven learning outcomes.
3. **My Role:** exact responsibilities and dates, what the owner decided/implemented/verified, collaborators and AI tool assistance.
4. **Key Features:** two or three confirmed working workflows, actual screenshots, anonymised inputs/outputs and known limitations. Separate implemented, experimental and planned work.
5. **Technical Approach:** actual technologies and why; one sanitised user-action/data-flow diagram. Omit keys, private URLs, paths and infrastructure.
6. **Engineering Challenges:** one observed failure → constraints → alternatives → decision → verification → residual limitation. Do not describe a proposed safeguard as implemented.
7. **Testing & Iteration:** test/evaluation scope, environment/version/date, result and limitations. Distinguish synthetic examples, native behaviour and actual model/provider evaluations. No metrics without an attributable record. One honest before/after iteration is enough to start.
8. **Current Development Status:** a dated milestone/version, working scope, known limits and next step. Distinguish development progress, release readiness and public availability.
